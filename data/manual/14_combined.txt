title: 14. Combined conditions
## 14.1 Arterial surgery
Heparin [i]before[/i] clamping. Clamp before opening. Repair the vessel before releasing the clamp.

## 14.2 Sympathomimetics
Cocaine and adrenaline together precipitate a cardiac event: arrest in {Patient.DangerArrestChance * 100}% of cases. After cocaine exposure, treat bradycardia with atropine and arrhythmia with amiodarone.

## 14.3 Heightened tactile sensitivity, conscious patient
Keep all contact within the operative field. Contact elsewhere causes sudden movement.

## 14.4 Alcohol use disorder with intraoperative awareness
Expect early and repeated lightening. Give propofol through the IV line at the first sign of speech.

## 14.5 Malignant hyperthermia with propofol allergy
Use ketamine. Never the inhaled agent.

## 14.6 Aneurysm with propofol allergy
Ketamine raises pressure. Induce with the inhaled agent. With malignant hyperthermia as well, use ketamine and keep the patient calm until they are under.

## 14.7 Aneurysm with anxiety disorder
Panic alone can rupture it. Sedate with diazepam before anything else.

## 14.8 Aneurysm with a coagulation disorder
• Mild hemophilia: a rupture bleeds {(quirk.coagulation_weak.bleed_mult - 1) * 100}% faster. Give tranexamic acid early and have clamps ready.
• Thrombophilia: heparin is still required, and every closure leaks while it acts. Clamp and cauterize bleeding points rather than packing them.

## 14.9 Diabetes with epilepsy
Low glucose and epilepsy both cause seizures. Diazepam stops them, but only glucose ends the cause: check the level after every seizure.
