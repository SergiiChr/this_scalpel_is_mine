## 6.1 Principle
A wound is closed only when the closure spans its entire length. Draw the instrument along the wound line while applying it.

A wound through the muscle is closed in layers. The cut muscle retracts and holds the wound open: sew it first, working inside the wound with the needle (or a stapler), along its whole length. Skin will not meet over open muscle, and a tight stitch pulled across it tears through. Adhesive strips cannot close muscle.

## 6.3 Suture tension
For sutures (and paper clips), tension uses the same three levels as blade pressure: the mouse wheel sets them, and the instrument works once lowered onto the wound ([b]Use tool[/b]). At level 0 no stitches are placed.
• Loose: rapid, but the wound edges do not meet and continue to ooze.
• Correct: edges apposed without blanching. The intended setting.
• Tight: strongest seal, but sutures may cut through. Thin skin tears at twice the usual rate.
> Traction on a sutured wound can pull sutures through.

## 6.4 Strain on the closure
Seizures, coughing, falls and impacts load the closure. Weak closures dehisce. Internal injuries (stomach, bowel, arteries) are repaired with sutures or cautery within the cavity.
