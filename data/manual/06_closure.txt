[b]6.1 Principle[/b]
A wound is closed only when the closure spans its entire length. Draw the instrument along the wound line while applying it.

[b]6.3 Suture tension[/b]
For sutures (and paper clips), tension uses the same three levels as blade pressure: with the instrument lowered onto the wound, the mouse wheel sets them. At level 0 no stitches are placed.
• Loose: rapid, but the wound edges do not meet and continue to ooze.
• Correct: edges apposed without blanching. The intended setting.
• Tight: strongest seal, but sutures may cut through. Thin skin tears at twice the usual rate.
Traction on a sutured wound can pull sutures through.

[b]6.4 Strain on the closure[/b]
Seizures, coughing, falls and impacts load the closure. Weak closures dehisce. Internal injuries (stomach, bowel, arteries) are repaired with sutures or cautery within the cavity.
