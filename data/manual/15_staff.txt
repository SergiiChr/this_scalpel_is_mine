title: 15. Operating team
## 15.1 Stress
Stress rises with errors, alarms and patient distress, and falls when the theatre is calm. At its limit the operator faints briefly and drops whatever is held.

## 15.2 Nausea
Nausea rises with exposure to blood (in susceptible staff), halitosis at close range and a colleague's vomiting. At its limit the operator vomits: turn away from the field.

## 15.3 Tremor
Stress shows in the hands. A calm operator only twitches now and then; past a third of the stress limit a light tremor reaches the instrument tip, and past two thirds it is plain to see. Cold shakes the hands too.
Holding the breath steadies the hands for as long as the breath lasts.
• [b]Diazepam for the operator:[/b] {drug.diazepam.Dose} mg per kg of the operator's own weight ({SurgeonStatus.BaseWeight} kg unless noted on the team sheet), injected into the back of the other hand or into a colleague. The tremor of stress settles within seconds and stays away for about {drug.diazepam.Duration / 60} minutes; the cold tremor does not. The cost: blurred vision and slower hands. Any other sedative or anaesthetic does the same at its own dose, and doses of different drugs add up.
• [b]Too much:[/b] past {DrugDef.DoseHigh} times the dose the vision darkens and the hands lag further. {SurgeonStatus.KnockoutShare} times the dose puts the operator on the floor for {SurgeonStatus.KnockoutTime / 60} minutes. [b]Flumazenil[/b] ({drug.flumazenil.Dose} mg per kg) reverses diazepam and [b]naloxone[/b] ({drug.naloxone.Dose} mg per kg) reverses morphine, but the operator stays down while the other drugs still hold them; the rest have no antidote. [b]Adrenaline[/b] gets them up while it lasts.
• [b]Morphine[/b] does not slow the hands. From {DrugDef.DoseEffective} times the dose the operator nods off instead: every {SurgeonStatus.NodGapMin} to {SurgeonStatus.NodGapMax} seconds the eyes close for {SurgeonStatus.NodDarkMin} to {SurgeonStatus.NodDarkMax} seconds.
• [b]Stimulants[/b] (adrenaline, or cocaine snorted from the baggie with the drink key): hands {SurgeonStatus.StimulantSpeed} times as fast, but stress builds {SurgeonStatus.StimulantStress} times as fast and the heart pounds. Both at once: hands and feet {SurgeonStatus.RaceSpeed} times as fast for {SurgeonStatus.RaceTime / 60} minutes, then the heart stops and the surgeon collapses. A partner has {SurgeonStatus.ArrestTime} seconds to shock their chest with the defibrillator; each shock has a {SurgeonStatus.ReviveChance * 100}% chance to restart the heart, which then slows back to normal over {SurgeonStatus.RecoverTime / 60} minute. Nobody in time and the surgeon dies. Once the whole team is dead, the operation is over.
• [b]Lidocaine into a hand[/b], any amount, numbs it for {SurgeonStatus.NumbTime} seconds: it drops what it holds and cannot grip.
• [b]Diphenhydramine[/b] takes nausea away while it works. An antidote with nothing to reverse only sets the heart pounding.

## 15.4 Perspiration
Sweat makes gloves slippery: change them at the glove box. A surgical cap prevents sweat dripping into an open wound.

## 15.5 Working together
• [b]Instrument handover:[/b] hold the instrument close to your colleague's empty hand and hand it over ([b]Grab[/b]). Both hands must be still, or it is dropped.
• [b]Crossing hands:[/b] two hands in the same space collide. Lift one over the other ([b]Lift[/b]), or agree on sides beforehand.
• [b]Traction while moving:[/b] a hand holding tissue stays where it is when you walk. Moving too far tears the tissue. Release before stepping away.
• [b]Changing hands:[/b] the hand you are not using stays exactly where it was and keeps doing what it was doing. A closed clamp stays closed; active cautery keeps burning.
