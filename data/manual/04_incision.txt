## 4.1 Blade pressure
Depth of the incision is set by blade pressure, not by the number of passes. There are three levels above rest. The current level is shown beside the aiming point whenever a blade is held, and on the status line.
[table=3]
[cell][b]Level[/b][/cell][cell][b]Divides[/b][/cell][cell][b]Use[/b][/cell]
[cell]1  Skin[/cell][cell]Epidermis and dermis[/cell][cell]Skin incision, scoring the line[/cell]
[cell]2  Fat[/cell][cell]Down to subcutaneous fat[/cell][cell]Deepening the wound under vision[/cell]
[cell]3  Muscle[/cell][cell]Fascia and muscle, into the cavity[/cell][cell]Entering the body cavity[/cell]
[/table]
[b]Setting it:[/b] lower the blade onto the skin ([b]Use tool[/b], default LMB). At level 0 it only rests there; the mouse wheel sets the depth, before or while it is down: forward increases, back decreases.
Start at level 1 and deepen in layers. A full-depth stroke from the outset gives no control over what lies beneath.

## 4.2 Technique
The aiming line shows the cutting edge on the skin. A blade divides tissue only when drawn along its edge; rotate the instrument to align the edge with the marked line. Drawn sideways it merely drags.
Draw the blade along the marked line in one steady stroke. Slow strokes leave clean edges; fast strokes leave ragged edges that bleed more. A dull or improvised blade always tears the edge.

## 4.3 Exposure
Skin is under tension and the edges part once divided. Beneath the skin lies yellow subcutaneous fat, then red muscle. The cavity is entered only when all three are divided.
To widen the exposure, grasp one edge with forceps or hook it with a retractor ([b]Use tool[/b]) and draw it laterally. Released ([b]Grab[/b]), the retractor lies down on the skin and keeps its traction; [b]Use tool[/b] unhooks it. To hold the wound open, lay a Gelpi retractor along the incision at its middle, its tips either side of it: the marks either side of the aiming point show where they enter. [b]Use tool[/b] sets it: the points sink into the incision as deep as it is divided, and on intact skin they glance off. The mouse wheel then opens it (forward) and closes it (back), and both edges follow. Released, it holds the wound open by itself and the deeper layers can be divided between its jaws; [b]Use tool[/b] again removes it. Excessive traction tears the skin and creates a second wound.

## 4.4 Inside the cavity
Dividing tissue next to a target frees it; dividing anything else injures it. Displace organs by hand before reaching beneath them; they return when released. Prolonged or forceful handling bruises viscera and causes oozing. Hold them with a retractor rather than by hand.
