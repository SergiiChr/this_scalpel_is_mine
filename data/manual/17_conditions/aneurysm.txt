title: Aneurysm
[i]A ballooned, thin-walled segment of a great vessel. Found under the chest, abdomen, thigh and neck.[/i]

[b]On the chart:[/b] "Known history of aneurysm."

[b]Hazards[/b]
• Systolic pressure above 140 mmHg can rupture it: sudden deep bleeding inside the operative field.
• Pressure rises with panic, adrenaline, cocaine and ketamine.

[b]Management[/b]
• Keep systolic pressure below 140 mmHg. Induce with propofol or the inhaled agent, not ketamine.
• Keep the conscious patient calm: reassure from the head of the table, or give diazepam.
• Give adrenaline only for arrest or anaphylaxis.
• A rupture is an internal injury: aspirate, clamp, then repair inside the cavity.
> High pressure also makes every closure leak (5.5). See Combined conditions (14.6 to 14.8).
