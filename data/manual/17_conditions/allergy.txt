title: Drug allergy
[i]A reaction to one of the standard agents, named on the chart or not.[/i]

[b]On the chart:[/b] “Allergic to cefazolin.”, “Allergic to morphine.”, “Allergic to lidocaine.”, “Allergic to propofol.” or “Claims a drug allergy but forgot which one.”

[b]Hazards[/b]
• The allergen causes urticaria and swelling, a fall in blood pressure and possibly anaphylaxis.

[b]Management[/b]
• When the chart doesn't name the agent, order the allergy screen from the laboratory to identify it.
• Use the alternative:
[table=3]
[cell][b]Allergy[/b][/cell][cell][b]Alternative[/b][/cell][cell][b]Caution[/b][/cell]
[cell]Cefazolin[/cell][cell]Clindamycin[/cell][cell]Longer delivery time[/cell]
[cell]Morphine[/cell][cell]Ketamine[/cell][cell]Raises heart rate and pressure[/cell]
[cell]Lidocaine[/cell][cell]General anesthesia[/cell][cell]Needs an IV line[/cell]
[cell]Propofol[/cell][cell]Ketamine or inhaled agent[/cell][cell]No inhaled agent with hyperthermia risk[/cell]
[/table]
• Urticaria and swelling: diphenhydramine.
• Falling pressure and oxygen saturation mean anaphylaxis: adrenaline immediately.
> See Combined conditions (14.5, 14.6).
