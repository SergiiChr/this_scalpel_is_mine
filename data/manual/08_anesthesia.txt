title: 8. Anesthesia management
## 8.1 Depth
Adequate depth is indicated by absent spontaneous respiration on the monitor. A patient who begins to speak is lightening.
One correct induction dose is maintained for the whole procedure. A repeat dose of the same agent only tops it up.

## 8.2 Blood pressure under anesthesia
Propofol and the inhaled agent lower systolic pressure for as long as the patient is under: expect about 100 mmHg instead of 120. A healthy patient tolerates this. Blood loss on top of it does not: below about 85 mmHg the heart is at risk of arrest.
• Control bleeding early.
• Run saline through the IV line to restore volume and pressure. Transfuse for larger losses.
• In a patient who is already bleeding or in shock, induce with ketamine. It keeps the pressure up.

## 8.3 Special cases
See Chronic conditions: Intraoperative awareness, Alcohol use disorder, Malignant hyperthermia.

## 8.4 Sequence
IV access, then induction, then skin preparation. Skin preparation may be done in the conscious patient; incision may not.
