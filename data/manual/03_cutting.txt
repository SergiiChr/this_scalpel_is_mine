title: Cutting and opening
[img=560]res://assets/manual/diagrams/incision_depth.svg[/img]

[b]Pressure[/b]
Light scratches the surface. Normal cuts skin. Deep goes through and opens the body.
Fat patients need deep pressure and a longer cut before anything opens.

[b]Speed[/b]
Slow strokes give clean edges. Fast strokes give jagged edges that bleed more. Dull blades (switchblade) always tear a little.

[b]Opening[/b]
A deep cut parts a little on its own. Grab an edge with forceps or a retractor and pull sideways to widen it. The skin tents toward the tool. Pull too far and it tears, which is its own wound now.

[b]Inside[/b]
Cutting near a target frees it. Cutting anything else nicks it. Push organs aside with your hand before you reach under them. They drift back when you let go.
Don't hold an organ out of the way for long, and don't shove it: after a few seconds it bruises and starts to ooze. Use a retractor or be quick.

[b]Regenerating patients[/b] close your incisions for you. Work in bursts, keep retractors in.
[b]Mirrored patients[/b]: everything inside is on the other side. Mark after you check.
