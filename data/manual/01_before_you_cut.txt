title: Before you cut
[img=520]res://assets/manual/diagrams/setup_order.svg[/img]

[b]1. IV line.[/b] Press the IV catheter onto an arm. Tubing then runs from the stand to the arm. Without a line, anything you give through the IV stand does nothing.
The tubing hangs across the floor space. Walk into it and you rip the line out of the arm. Crouch to step over it.

[b]2. Sanitize.[/b] Paint the site with the iodine swab until it's orange-brown. Unsanitized skin means infection later.

[b]3. Numb or sleep.[/b]
• [b]General:[/b] propofol (IV) or anesthetic gas mask. Wait for the monitor to show no respiration count, then start.
• [b]Local:[/b] lidocaine at the site. The patient stays awake and talks. Keep them calm.
• [b]None:[/b] when there's no time or nothing to give. Work fast, talk to them.

[b]Which one?[/b] The chart never tells you. Decide from what's wrong:
• A cut or a wound on an arm, a leg or the face, in a patient who is awake and can stay still: [b]local[/b].
• A child, or anyone too panicked to hold still: local plus diazepam, and one of you talks to them.
• Opening the belly, the chest or the skull, cutting bone, or anything that takes long: [b]general[/b].
• A heart that stopped or a patient bleeding out on the street: [b]none[/b]. Fix first.
• Awake and talking during brain surgery is the exception: put them under to open, let them surface for the tumor.

[b]4. Mark.[/b] Draw the line before you cut it. Marked incisions score better and keep you honest.

[b]Read the chart[/b], but on some nights the records get mixed up. If the lobby warned you about a chart mix-up, don't trust the blood type or allergies until the nurse brings the corrected copy.

Awake patients panic. Panicking patients thrash. Talk to them at the head of the bed, or sedate with diazepam.
