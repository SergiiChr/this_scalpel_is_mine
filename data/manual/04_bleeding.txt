title: Bleeding
The monitor shows total bleeding in ml/s. Anything above 1 is an emergency.

[b]In order of preference[/b]
1. [b]Pressure:[/b] press gauze on it. Buys time, fixes nothing.
2. [b]Clamp:[/b] hemostat on the vessel. Leave it on while you work.
3. [b]Cautery:[/b] seals small bleeders. Burns healthy tissue it touches.
4. [b]Close:[/b] sutures or staples along the whole wound.
5. [b]Tourniquet:[/b] limbs only. Stops nearly everything. Tissue starts dying after 5 minutes.

[b]Blood pools.[/b] On skin it hides the wound. Inside, it fills the cavity and hides the target. Suction first, then look.

[b]Drugs[/b]
• Tranexamic acid (TXA): blood clots faster. Less bleeding. More clot risk.
• Heparin: blood thins. Required before clamping arteries. More bleeding.
Never both. A patient with weak clotting bleeds nearly twice as fast. A patient with strong clotting may throw a clot and arrest unless you give heparin.
