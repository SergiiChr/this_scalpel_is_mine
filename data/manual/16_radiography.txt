title: 16. Intraoperative radiography
## 16.1 Indications
Localization of radiopaque foreign bodies (bullets, blades, retained instruments), assessment of bone, and a count check before closure.

## 16.2 Procedure
1. Take hold of the mobile unit anywhere ([b]Grab[/b]), like an instrument. It follows until released, and released it locks against the floor.
2. Position it beside the table. The collimator lights both fields on the patient: the frontal (AP) field straight below the tube head, the lateral field level and forward from just below it.
3. Raise or lower the arm with the [b]wheel[/b] while holding the unit.
4. Expose ([b]Use tool[/b]) while holding the unit. All staff stand clear during the exposure, which lasts a few seconds. Each exposure takes both views.
5. The two films are delivered into the cassette holder. They develop over about {XrayCart.DevelopTime} seconds.
6. Hold the films up ([b]Interact[/b]) against a light to read them. [b]Interact[/b] again puts them back.
> The unit requires {XrayCart.Cooldown} seconds between exposures.

## 16.3 Interpretation
The frontal film is read as if facing the patient: the patient's head at the top, the patient's right on the reader's left.
[table=2]
[cell][b]Appearance[/b][/cell][cell][b]Finding[/b][/cell]
[cell]Solid white, sharp edges[/cell][cell]Metal: projectiles, blades, instruments, other foreign bodies[/cell]
[cell]White, soft edges[/cell][cell]Bone, and glass. A break shows as a gap or a step in its outline[/cell]
[cell]Faint grey outline[/cell][cell]Body outline, organs, soft tissue masses (appendix, tumor, clot)[/cell]
[cell]Grey streak[/cell][cell]Wound track behind a projectile[/cell]
[cell]Black[/cell][cell]Air, outside the body and trapped inside it[/cell]
[/table]

## 16.4 Before closure
Take a film. A retained instrument will be found on the next film regardless of who takes it.
