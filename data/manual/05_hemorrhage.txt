title: 5. Hemorrhage control
## 5.1 Assessment
The monitor displays total blood loss rate in ml/s. A rate above {Patient.HeavyBleeding} ml/s is a surgical emergency.

## 5.2 Methods, in order of preference
1. [b]Direct pressure[/b] with gauze. Temporizing only.
2. [b]Clamping[/b] the vessel with a hemostat. Leave it in place while you work.
3. [b]Electrocautery[/b] for small bleeding points. Adjacent healthy tissue is coagulated as well.
4. [b]Closure[/b] of the full length of the wound with sutures or staples.
5. [b]Tourniquet[/b], limbs only. Press it onto the limb above the wound ([b]Use tool[/b]) and it is wrapped round and tightened. Near-complete control. Distal ischemia begins after {Patient.TourniquetSafe / 60} minutes of application in this facility's protocol. Take it off by picking it up.

## 5.3 Pooled blood
Blood on the skin obscures the wound; blood in the cavity obscures the target. Aspirate with suction or absorb with gauze before proceeding.

## 5.4 Pharmacological adjuncts
• [b]Tranexamic acid (TXA)[/b]: antifibrinolytic. Reduces bleeding; increases thrombotic risk.
• [b]Heparin[/b]: anticoagulant. Mandatory before arterial clamping. Increases bleeding.
> Do not combine.
See Chronic conditions: Mild hemophilia, Thrombophilia, Aneurysm.

## 5.5 Leaking closures
Sutures, staples, strips and packing hold only while the blood clots behind them. They leak while heparin acts, and while systolic pressure is above {Patient.HighPressure} mmHg. Cauterized points and clamps hold regardless.
Pressure rises with panic and with stimulants: adrenaline, cocaine, ketamine.
